1. Executive Summary
KrishiBodh
 is an AI-powered crop-health and risk-intelligence platform designed to support farmers and agricultural officers. The platform is intended to go beyond simple leaf-disease classification by combining crop images, pest-trap observations, farm and subplot information, weather, crop growth stage, farm history, nearby pest signals and follow-up observations.
The system first detects a crop disease or pest, then estimates risk, identifies potentially affected neighboring subplots, provides an actionable advisory, verifies the situation through follow-up observations, and escalates important cases to an agriculture officer. Regional signals can then be aggregated anonymously to support hotspot intelligence.
The documentation emphasizes a practical prototype: large datasets should not be downloaded or trained blindly. Instead, manageable subsets, transfer learning, lightweight 
inference,
 and a transparent demo fallback should be used when full ML training is not immediately practical.
2. Problem Statement
Farmers may notice disease or pest symptoms only after crop damage becomes significant. A system that only identifies a disease from a single image does not provide enough context to understand farm-level risk, neighboring subplot pressure, weather conditions, historical trends or what action should be taken next.
KrishiBodh
 addresses this gap by combining multiple sources of evidence into one connected decision-support platform.
3. Project Objectives
Detect crop diseases and insect pests from images.
Record pest-trap observations as time-series data.
Represent farms as manageable subplots such as P1–P9.
Combine pest pressure, weather, crop stage, history and spatial proximity into a transparent risk score.
Predict elevated pest risk in neighboring subplots within configurable monitoring windows.
Provide structured, expert-reviewed recommendations instead of unrestricted AI-generated chemical instructions.

7. Dataset and Model Sources
The project documentation identifies three main data sources. These sources serve different purposes and should not be treated as one combined dataset without careful preprocessing and leakage checks.
IP102 — Primary Pest Detection Dataset
GitHub: 
https://github.com/xpwu95/IP102
Used for insect-pest recognition. It contains 75,000+ pest images, 102 pest 
categories,
 and approximately 19,000 images with bounding-box annotations. For the prototype, use only classes relevant to supported crops and a manageable subset with transfer learning. IP102 should be used according to its stated academic-use terms and should not be presented as unrestricted commercial data.
SAGE — Primary Crop Disease Dataset
Hugging Face: 
https://huggingface.co/datasets/tirtho149/SAGE
Used primarily for crop-disease recognition. The documented dataset contains 820,504 images, 335 crops and 1,251 crop × disease classes, with plant-organ, symptom, pathogen, disease-type and provenance information. Use streaming or selected subsets rather than downloading the complete dataset into the repository.
Digital Green Crop Disease Images — Real-World Validation
Hugging Face: 
https://huggingface.co/datasets/DigiGreen/Crop_Disease_Images
Used primarily for validation and realistic farmer-image testing. The documented source contains 989 photographs, 1,026 expert annotations and 74 crop types, with India-heavy coverage. It should not be the only training source.
8. Dataset Handling Strategy
Do not download or train on massive datasets blindly.
Select supported crops and relevant disease/pest classes.
Create a reproducible subset for the prototype.
Keep TRAIN
, 
VALIDATION,
 and TEST data clearly separated.
Avoid data leakage where images or source datasets overlap.
Record class names, mappings, preprocessing and dataset attribution.
Do not commit datasets or model weights into Git.

22. Model Fallback and Demo Mode
The application should remain runnable when trained models are unavailable. It should expose a real model inference interface and a transparent demo/mock inference mode, for example AI_PROVIDER=model or AI_PROVIDER=demo.
The UI should indicate demo/model mode where appropriate. The project must never claim that a model was trained when it is not actually available.

27. Conclusion
KrishiBodh
 is designed as one connected crop-health and risk-intelligence platform rather than a collection of independent features. Its main value comes from connecting detection with subplot-level pest observations, contextual risk assessment, actionable advisory, follow-up 
verification,
 and officer validation. The prototype should prioritize a reliable end-to-end workflow, transparent risk logic, responsible AI 
behavior,
 and clear separation between real observations and predictions.
Every developer should think: “I am building one part of 
KrishiBodh
, not a separate application.” Frontend, backend, AI, risk engine and officer portal must communicate through clearly defined contracts.
